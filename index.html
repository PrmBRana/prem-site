Earthquake Precursor Detection Mission (EPDM) - Quad-Mag Technology
Jan 2020 - Aug 2020

The Earthquake Precursor Detection Mission (EPDM) is designed to use a Quad-Mag technology payload, consisting of four PNI RM3100 magnetometers, to detect and analyze electromagnetic signals in the Ultra-Low Frequency (ULF: below 3 Hz) and Extremely Low Frequency (ELF: 3 Hz to 3 kHz) ranges, which are generated from the Earth’s surface before an earthquake. Unlike traditional satellites that rely on long telescopic or deployable booms to protect magnetometers from stray magnetic fields caused by internal electric subsystems, the EPDM employs Quad-Mag technology and the Undetermined Blind Source Separation (UBSS) algorithm, both tested in the University of Michigan lab to remove interference or stray magnetic field and accurately measure the Earth’s magnetic field. The PNI RM3100 magnetometers, proven suitable for CubeSat missions, operate with high efficiency and are capable of functioning in space environments. The primary objective of the mission is to demonstrate the capability of space-based technologies to separate stray magnetic fields from ambient magnetic fields for detecting earthquake precursor signals without using any mechanical boom.

Magnetometer Calibration for EPDM Payload - Thin-Shell Calibration
Feb 2016 – Mar 2017

The Thin-Shell Calibration method was applied to the EPDM payload’s magnetometers to improve measurement accuracy by compensating for sensor imperfections such as variable sensitivities (gains), constant offsets (biases), and slight misalignments.

This calibration approach determines nine correction parameters —
three for scaling, three for offsets, and three for angular misalignments — and applies them to the raw data to transform the measured points from an ellipsoidal to a spherical distribution.

The calibration referenced known magnetic field strengths from both a scalar magnetometer and the International Geomagnetic Reference Field (IGRF) model.

Validation Tests:

  • Site 1: Budhanilkantha School (27°46′46″ N, 85°21′35″ E; 1450 m), IGRF = 49,068.8 nT
  • Site 2: Pulchowk Field (27°40′60″ N, 85°19′20″ E; 1280 m), IGRF = 49,015.8 nT

At both sites, the raw three-axis magnetometer data initially showed scattered points. After applying the Thin-Shell Calibration method, the corrected results aligned closely with the IGRF reference values, confirming the robustness and precision of the calibration technique.

This calibration was performed as part of the Earthquake Precursor Detection Mission (EPDM) payload validation to ensure accurate magnetic field readings for space-based anomaly detection.

Slippers2Sat
Nepal's First Middle School Nano-Satellite Project — Slippers2Sat (S2S).******** Slippers2Sat (S2S) is a 1U CubeSat project by Antarikchya Pratisthan Nepal (APN) with support from Amateur Radio Digital Communications (ARDC). Developed by six APN engineers, the project trains students at the Space System Laboratory (SSL) in Navodaya School through hands-on satellite development. In early 2024, the APN team conducted an Amateur Radio and Satellite Bootcamp in Lamjung, Makawanpur, and Chitwan, selecting 10 students from Chepang, Tamang, and Dalit communities to participate.
